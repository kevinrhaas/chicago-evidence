HOW MANY SLEPT HERE

12 on an ordinary night · 27 when full RECONSTRUCTED

The town model puts a boarding house at 9 people on an ordinary night and 21 when full, from the 1840 household tail (p90 and p99 of 964 Chicago households). This building takes the share of its class's total that its 263 m² of enclosed floor (131 m² × 2 storeys) is of the class's floor area.

why

6 slept here on this card · 6 beds empty

6 on this card against an ordinary night's 12 beds: 5 seated here from elsewhere in the layer, where no source gives them a roof, a keeper drawn for a roof this programme raised.

This house has a division and empty beds, and the reconstruction plan has nothing left to draw them out of: every adult `lodging/none` cell of the north division is filled to what the book orders there. A person minted past that would be a person the town never ordered, so the beds stand empty and the order rather than the roof is what is short.

The keeper's own household stands at 4 people. Minted by this stage as the keeper. Other work drew this keeper's own children here — 3 of them — out of the book's under 10 lodging cells, at the size the 1840 histogram gives the household. What is still owed is a SPOUSE, which no rule this project has made can draw for a keeper who is a woman; the ledger's keeper families row states it, and the staffing under a keeper is other work's.
