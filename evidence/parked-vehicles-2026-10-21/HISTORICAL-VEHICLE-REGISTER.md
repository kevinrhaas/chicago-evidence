## The Dearborn passage, verified against actual1856 pages

**Author:** Juliette Augusta Magill Kinzie, published as Mrs. John H. Kinzie. Her husband in the passage is **John Harris Kinzie**, not his father John Kinzie the elder, who died in1828 and is the separate1812 study identity.

**Book:** *Wau-Bun: The “Early Day” in the North-West*. New York: Derby & Jackson,1856. IA `waubunearlydayin00kinz_0`; this is a retrieved1856 copy. Existing source ID `kinzie_waubun_1856` can be reused with an expanded locator; it currently names only chapterXVII. The local Gutenberg EPUB is a later1873 printing and was used as a finding aid before the1856 scan was acquired.

**ChapterXXIII, “Second Sight—Hickory Creek,” printed p297, scan filename0309:**

> Our heavy articles of furniture, trunks, &c., had been sent on board the Napoleon to be brought round to us by way of Fox River. We had retained only such few necessaries as could be conveniently carried on a pack-horse, and in a light dearborn wagon lately brought by Mr. Kercheval from Detroit (the first luxury of the kind ever seen on the prairies), and which my husband had purchased as an agreeable mode of conveyance for his mother and little nephew.

**ChapterXXIV, “Return to Fort Winnebago,” printed p302, scan filename0314:**

> Our wagon had been provided with a pair of excellent travelling horses, and sister Margaret and myself accommodated with the best pacers the country could afford, and we set off in high spirits toward the Aux Plaines ...

The image, not only OCR, was read for both excerpts. Narrative sequence belongs to the1831 Chicago visit (chapterXVII explicitly “Chicago in1831”; chapterXXI describes severe weather through the second week of April before departure). No exact departure day/month is asserted from these pages. “First luxury ... ever seen on the prairies” is the author's recollection, not a verified first-invention/import record.

A later *Webster's1913* definition, actually read at `https://www.websters1913.com/words/Dearborn`, calls a dearborn “A four-wheeled carriage, with curtained sides.” Modern Wiktionary defines a light four-wheel wagon and does not establish an inventor's identity. These clarify that the word is a vehicle type; the1913 entry is NOT contemporary1831 proof of this wagon's curtain/roof details. Neither source identifies a manufacturer. The1828 dictionary host lookup did not return an actual entry and is not evidence.

### Practical Dearborn modeling specification

- Build a **light passenger wagon**, visually distinct from W01's deep freight box. Its function carries an adult woman, child and a few necessities, with heavy trunks sent by ship.
- For a trip reconstruction, use the expressly described **pair of horses** and therefore a pair-team arrangement; engineering dimensions and exact pole/hitch still need independently sourced/reconstructed design. Do not call a single-horse shaft rig the exact Kinzie trip.
- Make roof/curtains optional pending a dated early comparator. Do not infer a high prairie-schooner canvas hoop roof from the word wagon.
- Preserve portable wheels/front running-gear/tongue/seats/driver/passenger frames; do not copy W01 body geometry and merely relabel it.
- Record all invented dimensions, paint, joinery, upholstery and exact placement. A named reconstruction can be compelling without pretending to be an archival likeness.
- Before calling it photographic, render actual side/front-quarter/rear-quarter views in the app and compare against an identified eligible construction comparator. The source paragraph itself is not an image or a measured drawing.
