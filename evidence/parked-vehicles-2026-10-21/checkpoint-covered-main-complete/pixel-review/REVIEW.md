## Visible improvements and preserved differences

- In both desktop pairs the low-sided polygonal canopy becomes a much smoother bowed outline with visible panel/seam lines and the front bow visible inside the opening. Broad stripe-like shading on the old angular roof is substantially reduced. The severe regular ribbing reported in the earlier standalone gallery is not conspicuous in these actual main frames; this is an observation under these saved production-light views, not a universal shadow qualification.
- Western retains a dark blue body, red/oxide running gear and pale cover. Wolf Point retains an oak/brown body with darker brown gear. Both differences remain readily apparent on desktop and mobile. The source metadata separately records distinct retained body and canvas aging scalars and exact original finish-seed strings. This review read those declarations; it did not independently rerun the seed contract or establish that the small aging difference is visually separable across different lighting/contexts.
- The wagon stands and bearings look stable within each old/new desktop pair: fence, background and tongue framing remain aligned. No extra old copy beside the replacement is evident in the reviewed pixels. This is not a new runtime duplicate-count or coordinate audit.
- The mobile Western close frame resolves the blue boards, wheel spokes/hubs, front opening and seams well enough to show a coherent upgrade. It also exposes the remaining simplified finish. Existing surrounding wagons are still present; the unupgraded blue covered example at the right of the Wolf Point desktop scene is not evidence that both new variants are clones.

The two new models still share their overall cover and body architecture. Color families alone should not be used later to claim distinct historical manufacturers or models. Preserving the current deliberate differences is successful within these visible frames; stronger geometric variety should follow actual evidence or explicitly declared reconstruction rather than random reshaping.

## Remaining photographic gaps, in priority order

1. **Cloth construction and close surface response.** The canopy still reads as a very regular manufactured arch with repeated panel divisions, long smooth stretches, some visible planar/triangular shading in the close view, and a uniformly sharp lower edge. Seam marks are orderly and prominent relative to the sparse fine fabric response. First resolve a cleared period construction reference for bow spacing, cloth support, hems, openings and fastening; then add only the supported or explicitly reconstructed tension, restrained folds, edge thickness and attachment detail. These frames do not prove any particular flap, tie or historical tailoring is required. Do not substitute arbitrary wrinkle noise for construction.
2. **Wood, paint and iron detail at close distance.** Broad pale horizontal streaks make the boards look patterned, while wheels/spokes/hubs and much of the gear retain smooth, relatively uniform surfaces and simple edges. Improve scale-correct wood/paint breakup and restrained iron roughness, wear and fastening detail where a source or declared reconstruction supports it. Keep the blue/oxide and oak/darkoak identities. These screenshots are insufficient to judge dimensions or identify a historical maker.
3. **Evidence-bounded individual aging and silhouette variety.** The finish distinction is strong; canopy aging is subtle and not independently comparable under these different light/locations. Before extending to the other covered stands, inspect several variants under one matched light and pose so stable per-ID canvas wear, board variation and coherent construction differences remain visible without reassigning identities. No blanket fleet replacement is justified by this seven-image snapshot.
4. **Complete the currently missing angles and delivery checks.** Rear/side/underbody and distant representations, source versus published visual agreement, compressed near/mid/far outputs and both upgraded variants in one comparison remain open in this review. Full resolution and in-scene rendering should be judged together; gallery polish alone is insufficient.
