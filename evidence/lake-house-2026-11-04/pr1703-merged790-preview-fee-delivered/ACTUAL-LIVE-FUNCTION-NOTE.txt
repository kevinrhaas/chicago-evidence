The building's purpose is documented and its completion date is documented, and between them they make the scene-date function a construction site rather than a hotel. Andreas: 'Chicago did, however, have a really grand hotel as early as 1835; this was the Lake House ... The hotel was completed and thrown open to the public in the autumn of 1836, Jacob Russel, of Middleton, Conn., assuming control of its management.' The men behind it are documented too and are worth carrying because they are the town's commercial leadership rather than innkeepers: Gurdon S. Hubbard, General David S. Hunter, John H. Kinzie, Dr W. B. Egan and Major James B. Campbell - 'the men whose enterprise led them into building a house which for those days was far in advance of the needs of the town'.

AND THE SCENE-YEAR PRESS DATES THE SITE, WHICH ANDREAS DOES NOT. 'There is [a] new house building in Kinzie's addition [to be] named we understand, the "Lake Hous[e]."' — the Chicago Democrat of 10 June 1835, page 2 column 5, claim chicago_democrat_1835_06_10#c001, the same paragraph that reports the Sauganash freshly refitted. Both halves are load-bearing. IT IS A BUILDING SITE three weeks before the scene date, north of the main branch, which is what makes hotel_under_construction an attested function at 1835-07-01 rather than a back-projection from the 1836 opening. AND ITS NAME IS STILL SECOND-HAND — 'we understand' — so on the scene date the town has an unfinished building that people are calling the Lake House and not a hotel with a sign. The same paragraph contains an editorial proposal for a house 'about double the size of that latter' near the centre of Lake Street on this side of the river. The comparison expresses a desired relative hotel size; it supplies no measured dimensions or July construction stage and does not establish the Lake House's rank among existing buildings. The proposal is not an existing-house observation and does not prove that the suggested site was vacant.

Fergus's 1876 recollection of the 1839 hotel is included here only for later establishment identity and the conflicting Rush/North Water address, not as evidence of the July 1835 construction stage, position or dimensions. Its Hotels entry was directly checked at the printed page; the existing contemporary project notice remains the evidence for work before the scene date.
