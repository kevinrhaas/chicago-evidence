## P1 — primary1833 American publication: construction actually read

**Lebeaud, The principles of the art of modern horsemanship for ladies and gentlemen: in which all late improvements are applied to practice.** Philadelphia: E.L.Carey & A.Hart, **1833**. IA metadata actually read: https://archive.org/metadata/principlesofarto00lebe . Actual OCR text read: https://archive.org/stream/principlesofarto00lebe/principlesofarto00lebe_djvu.txt . Metadata creator “Lebeaud, d.1832”; `rights` field absent. **Reasoned US public-domain assessment from Philadelphia1833 publication**, not an explicit host license grant. No photograph/illustration pixels derived.

Printed **pp.25–27**, *Of the Saddle, and its different parts*:

- Text enumerates saddle bows, sidebars, pommel (“pummel”), seat, panels (“pannels”), quarters and girth-leathers; accessory crupper, breast leather, girths and stirrup straps. Historical “reins” in the rear-body anatomical context can mean loins; do not misread it as leather reins passing under the seat.
- **p.26:** saddle bows are described as two pieces of **beech wood**, formed around the back, supporting other parts. Front bow has pommel, sides and points. Rear bow is wider/rounder; French saddle “troussequin” is described separately. Two wooden sidebars connect bows. The text gives sidebar width as “three or four fingers”: this is **not** a metric dimension and no conversion is asserted.
- **pp.26–27:** panels are **two cushions of linen stuffed with hair**, including cow/deer hair. Supports a pair of padded bearing surfaces rather than one solid slab resting on the spine. Material claim applies to the construction described, not all saddles.
- **pp.28–29** nearby text describes relatively unobstructed English-form saddles and a rear support when carrying a portmanteau. Actual paragraph read says “such are the English saddles”; exact omitted-page interpretation needs page-image collation before precision claims about a particular pattern. No horn is attested by these read passages.
- **pp.29–30**, saddling: girths/stirrup straps lifted out of the way before saddle placement; saddle placed/adjusted on horse; front bow described at **three finger breadths** from shoulders. Do not convert this historical fit advice into a universal numeric safe clearance or veterinary certification. Body fit still requires actual model contact/clearance checks.

Printed **pp.15–16**, *Of the Bridle and Bit*: English bridles are distinguished with four reins plus snaffle independently of the bit, rather than silently assuming every bridle is one pair of reins. Bit forms and mouthpiece are discussed separately. This is evidence that double-bridle and simpler arrangements need explicit selection; it does **not** instruct the first original civilian fixture to use every component simultaneously.

Printed contents identify **pp.41ff** mounting/sitting and **pp.73ff** ladies' instructions. Those headings alone are not a plate observation or an excuse to attach a side-saddle to a straddle pose. A first straddle-seat assembly and a later side-saddle are separate patterns and acceptance work.

**What remains absent:** actual saddle illustration/cut/object views, exact tree/panel lengths, leather cut patterns, stirrup shape and full assembly proportions. This source supports an original component graph and historical materials at declared reconstruction dimensions; it does not yet support a photo-matched master.

## P2 — Chicago1902 retail pattern, actually read

**Sears, Roebuck and Co., Catalogue No.111, Spring1902**, metadata date **1902-03**, Chicago commercial catalog. Metadata: https://archive.org/metadata/sears-roebuck-catalogue-111 . Actual OCR: https://archive.org/stream/sears-roebuck-catalogue-111/Sears%20Roebuck%20Catalogue%20111_djvu.txt . IA metadata has no explicit rights/license field. US public-domain assessment rests on the1902 published catalog; this is our rationale, not a host declaration. Stable item numbers are supplied because printed page imagery/pagination was not inspected.

### Saddle10R1350 — preferred1904 civilian starting design

Heading **“Men's $4.35 Plain English Saddle”**; actual text read:

> “fair leather only … Somerset tree, pigskin covered seat, seamed seat and jockey, pigskin impression skirts, full padded, cotton flannel lined, 1-inch stirrup leathers, Super cotton girth, 3-inch wood stirrups.”

Tree-length fraction is OCR **`15%-inch`** and is deliberately **withheld**. Do not normalize it to15.5in without the page. The **1-inch stirrup-leather width** (25.4mm) and **3-inch wood-stirrup** description (76.2mm) are legible text readings; the latter's exact dimension axis needs the illustration/object verified before setting an internal foot opening. “Fair leather” is the catalog's finish term, not a recovered color from a monochrome photo. Pigskin “impression” skirt is distinct from an actual pigskin-covered seat; do not assign the same microstructure to both without reasoning.

### Saddle10R1345 — distinct alternative

Heading **“Men's $2.50 Plain English Saddle”**; text gives russet or black leather, full leather-covered seat, fancy pigskin-impression skirts, full padded, sheepskin face, drill lining, cotton girth and wooden stirrups. Tree length and stirrup-leather width fractions are OCR-corrupted and withheld. This cheaper construction is not interchangeable with1350's pigskin seat/cotton-flannel lining while remaining “attested to1350”.

### Bridle10R1803 — simplest concrete1904 companion

Heading **“Flat Snaffle”**: flat russet-leather head and reins with bar buckles, headstall/reins sewn into a **full-cheek snaffle bit**. The exact buckle designation and fractional strap widths are OCR uncertain. Reconstruction can use flat head/rein straps, a named separate throat/crown/cheek graph and original full-cheek bit geometry, with all unverified shapes/dimensions declared reconstructed. Do not use a curb/Pelham mouthpiece while labeling it1803.

Adjacent items are **different**:10R1805 is described under flat-snaffle heading but text mentions a port bit/curb strap (possible catalog terminology or OCR issue—resolve from page);10R1811 is explicitly flat Pelham with two reins;10R1810 is web halter-riding bridle; racing items1807/1809 have separate bit supply. Avoid treating these as optional parts of one definitive1803 kit.

### Chronology exclusions

The1902 catalog also lists McClellan, USArmy and horned/Morgan saddle patterns. Their presence is1902 evidence only. No1860s military type or modern Western silhouette is backdated into1835/1812; likewise no absence claim is made that horned civilian saddles never existed earlier. The current light driving-harness “saddle”/pad, check-rein terrets and traces are not this riding saddle or its rein system. ## P3 —1805 pre1812 text, separated from1833

**John Adams, riding master, An analysis of horsemanship**, volume1, London: Albion Press/James Cundee, **1805**, IA `analysisofhorsem01adam`. Metadata actually read and actual OCR read: https://archive.org/stream/analysisofhorsem01adam/analysisofhorsem01adam_djvu.txt . Age-based US public-domain assessment; no explicit metadata rights field. This is British riding instruction before1812, not a Chicago equipment inventory.

Actual text **pp.26–27**, *On the Hand*: distinguishes single-rein bridle arrangements such as snaffles from other bit/bridoon arrangements and describes reins held together in the hand. A separate passage warns to check saddle placement, girths and bridle before mounting. This supports the existence of an early snaffle/rein civilian comparator and basic component graph, **not** exact saddle cut, stirrup shape or an eligible1812 person's equipment.

Do not backdate the1833 edition to1812. An1812 assembly needs separately sourced pre1812 geometry/construction (Adams later volumes or dated artifacts/plates are leads, not read evidence yet), and a date-qualified person record. Scene target is1812-08-14. No complete1812 tack pattern is established by this pass.
