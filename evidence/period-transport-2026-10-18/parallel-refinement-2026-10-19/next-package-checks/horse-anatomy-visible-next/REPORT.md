# Visible horse anatomy: three major corrections

2026-10-19. Actually opened BOTH current desktop renders and ALL SIX retained Cuyer plates. Image paths/hashes/dimensions in image-view-receipt.json. No generated images/model/app edits or network. Cuyer1905 is already documented US-PD anatomical analogy, not photographic surface/breed/Chicago identity evidence. Current renders are oblique and animated, plates diagrammatic; no metric or projected-ratio measurements are justified. The visible deficiency is much larger than harness microclearance.

## 1. Head/ear/neck silhouette — first correction parcel

Current desktop-side-saddle-support.png: two tall, nearly straight parallel narrow ear extrusions rise from a simple rounded crown. Desktop-rearquarter.png shows both ears as thick upright leaf/paddle silhouettes with very little differentiated base/cup. Head has a small isolated eye bead, near-straight wedge muzzle and little modeled cheek/jaw transition. Neck joins head/shoulder as a broad, angular solid sheet; underside is nearly straight into chest, with weak distinction of jaw/throatlatch and shoulder.

Actual reference illo262.png, Cuyer Fig92,p239: ear has distinct base and narrowing tip/outer contour; cheek is dominated by a large masseter volume behind the mouth; jaw's underside turns into the throat rather than merging into an unbroken slab; muzzle is a separate soft rounded region with visible nostril opening and lip line. illo169.png Fig70,p146 gives sloped neck crest, different dorsal/ventral contours, and neck-to-scapula overlap; illo086.png Fig40,p64 exposes curved cervical chain rather than a single straight tapered block.

**Action:** prioritize a coherent head/ear parcel, starting ear mesh audit against source values: replace uniform deep extrusions with thinner tapered leaf shells, narrower insertion/base, shallow inner concavity and separate bilateral ear rotations; reduce the current dominating upright silhouette by an explicitly reconstructed design choice, not a plate-derived species ratio. Rebuild head cross-sections to separate forehead/nasal bridge, rounded muzzle, broad rear cheek/masseter and inset eye surround; retain bit/bridle anchors with fit recheck. Shape a visible jaw-to-throatlatch transition and curved crest/neck sections next, rather than shrinking the whole head or changing muzzle/ear dimensions indiscriminately. Do NOT copy exposed muscle lines into skin grooves. Fig92 is a close-up and cannot supply an ear:whole-body ratio; no asserted numeric ear length here.

**Acceptance:** same current two cameras plus clean near-profile/front head views; ear should read as a cupped tapered anatomical form and cheek/muzzle as separate volumes before adding texture. Source comparison qualitative, not photoacceptance. Keep existing rig/gear anchors stable unless measured fit requires a separately reviewed change.

## 2. Continuous limb anatomy, joints and hooves — second parcel

Both current images show long nearly constant-width straight polygon tubes ending in visible ball-like ankle beads and broad flat grey hoof caps. Forelimb upper segment transition is abrupt; the near hind upper limb resembles an angular block attached to the barrel. Thin lower legs are expected in horses, but uniform tubes plus sphere joints erase carpal/hock/fetlock distinctions. Animated phase/obliquity prevents judging actual bone lengths from these screenshots.

Actual illo205.png Fig75,p182: muscular upper forearm tapers; carpus is a localized structured transition; cannon is slender and distinct; fetlock projects behind, pastern slopes into a sloped hoof. illo243.png Fig86,p220: hind leg has a prominent rear hock projection with tendon route and a long slender lower cannon, not a copy of the fore knee. illo086.png Fig40,p64 confirms different fore/hind joint chains. illo279.png Fig100,p256 shows actual hoof underside wall/sole/frog plan, absent from a flat disc.

**Action:** replace ball beads and constant tube profiles with continuous anatomical section transitions: forearm belly→carpus→cannon→rear fetlock→sloped pastern; separate hind thigh/stifle/tapered lower leg→rear hock→cannon chain. Preserve articulated joints and no-scale rig, but overlap/deform envelopes so rigid-section seams do not open. Hoof should be a wall with heel/toe and sloped profile/sole; underside frog can follow after major silhouette. Do not shorten/reposition bones based on these perspective renders. Verify actual rest chain then gait/ground contacts after any articulation change.

## 3. Rump/tail volume and attachment — third parcel

Current desktop-rearquarter.png shows the tail as a broad flat dark blade/ribbon attached high on a faceted rump; side render shows angular bent strip and thin pointed end. The extreme orientation-dependent thickness is a visible geometry issue, not a request for texture detail. Rump/upper hind limb transition also reads as separate hard slabs.

Actual illo169.png Fig70,p146 depicts rounded hindquarter muscle masses and a distinct tail root descending from the croup; illo086.png Fig40,p64 shows a segmented caudal skeletal chain extending from sacrum, then bending down, not a flat plate. These plates DO NOT show individual hair-fiber density/photographic tail groom, so a specific groom cannot be claimed source-matched.

**Action:** model a short rounded tapering dock with a coherent croup insertion, then a volumetric tapered hair envelope or multiple crossed/curved hair surfaces that retain width/depth from rearquarter and side. Round the croup-to-thigh muscle transition without inflating the entire barrel. Keep tail collision/gait checks separate. Hair density, length and color remain reconstruction until a licensed photographic reference is acquired.

## Scope and order

Recommend one bounded first revision of **ear/head silhouette** (root/model agent to choose precise scope), then limb topology, then rump/tail. All three are substantial geometric work; fine material roughness or trace clearance cannot compensate. This review does not declare the horse photoreal, anatomically accepted or period-breed identified. Current harness contacts and animation should be rerun after each adopted shape change; avoid bundling all changes into one unreviewable update.
