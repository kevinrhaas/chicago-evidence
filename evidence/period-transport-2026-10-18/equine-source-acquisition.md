## Acquired and read: anatomy with explicit public-domain designation

**George Stubbs, The anatomy of the horse**, Internet Archive `b33544049`, contributed/sponsored by Wellcome Library.

- Metadata actually read: https://archive.org/metadata/b33544049
- Full OCR actually read: https://archive.org/stream/b33544049/b33544049_djvu.txt
- Record date: **1815**, described as original text sheets reissued with plates reprinted on wove paper watermarked 1815/1; 4 unnumbered pages, 47 pages, 24 leaves of plates. Do not label this copy simply the 1766 edition or an 1815 eyewitness Chicago source.
- Metadata rights field explicitly says: **“This work is available under the Creative Commons, Public Domain Mark”**, linking `http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/`. This is the strongest cleared anatomy basis found in this pass. A PDM is a public-domain designation, not a new creative license.
- OCR passages actually read identify the scapula and muscles attaching to it; humerus upper articulation with the scapular cavity; radius/ulna and the olecranon receiving space; hind limb patella, tibia/fibula, astragalus and calcaneus, followed by other tarsal bones. The text describes the calcaneal tubercle/tendon attachment and ligaments between tibia and tarsus.
- Concrete modeling use: forelimb shoulder/upper-arm/forearm articulation must not be replaced with a straight stick; the hind stifle and hock are separate joints and the hock projection has a bony/tendon basis. A bone-naming/parentage map can cite this text. This pass did **not** measure segment lengths or claim matching skeletal proportions from unread plates.
- OCR is heavily damaged by long-s typography and page layout. Read passages support structure/terminology, not numeric anatomy. Do not copy an OCR-mangled anatomical label or inferred dimension into a production schema without the page image.
- Official cover thumbnail returned 200 but supplied no useful anatomical plate. The scanned plate set remains **unseen**. Required next step: fetch and inspect side/front/rear skeletal and muscle plates, save exact leaf/plate locators and confirm read interpretations, then compare a sculpt. No claim of photographic-quality anatomy acceptance yet.

## Acquired and read: American period harness construction/material recipe

**John Moore (Firm: New York), Fine harness, saddlery & horse clothing**, **1891**, New York; Columbia University Libraries copy, IA `fineharnesssaddl1891john`.

- Metadata: https://archive.org/metadata/fineharnesssaddl1891john
- Full OCR actually read: https://archive.org/stream/fineharnesssaddl1891john/fineharnesssaddl1891john_djvu.txt
- Catalog masthead identifies John Moore, 53, 55, 57 and 59 Warren Street, New York, and 1891. This is an American catalog pre-1904, not proof of any named Chicago owner's purchases or of 1835 practice.
- IA metadata has no `rights` or `licenseurl` field. **Rights conclusion is this audit's reasoned US public-domain assessment from the printed 1891 US publication date, not an institutional rights declaration.** Confirm the actual publication/imprint image and record that basis before deriving production assets. No picture-derived asset was made here.

Printed page **1**, heading **BUGGY HARNESS**, No. 1, OCR read:

> “Nickel or Davis Rubber trimmings … folded and stitched breast collar, breeching and girth, straight stitched 1 inch traces, flat reins, neat bridle, with round check rein, plated front”

Concrete modeling consequences: one supported light buggy configuration has a breast collar, breeching and girth as distinct components; traces are stated as 1 inch (25.4 mm), and reins are described as flat while the check rein is round. Rubber trim is a period option, so shiny metal everywhere is not mandatory. The harness “saddle” is harness equipment; it is not evidence for a riding saddle.

Printed page **8**, heading **Village Cart Harness**, No. 52, OCR read: square or English-style winkers, round side check rein, plated front/rosettes, nickel bit, **Tilbury tugs**, patent leather collar and flat russet reins. Some fractional widths are corrupted in OCR and are **withheld** pending the image; do not convert `3J^-inch` or `li^-inch` to an invented measurement.

Printed page **13**, heading **American Style of Coach Harness**, No. 80, OCR read: short-tug coach harness, nickel trim, English-style coach winkers, patent-leather collars, matching hames, hip straps and patent-leather trace bearers, folded girths, flat reins. Trace-width fraction is OCR-corrupt and withheld. This is a **separate collar/hames configuration**, not an instruction to put breast collar and every coach component on the first buggy harness.

Other text actually read describes silver, German-silver, brass/nickel and hard-rubber trimming variants, black or russet reins, folded/stitched layers and different finishes. These support material-family and construction options for a bounded reconstruction; they do not recover color or specific hardware on the Chrimes photograph.

**Remaining limitation:** illustrations, exact buckle shapes, routing around the horse and uncertain fractional dimensions were not viewed. Use text as construction evidence; do not claim a traced or photo-matched harness.

## Acquired and read: 1904 harness-making manual

**Paul N. Hasluck, editor, Harness making**, **1904**, Philadelphia: D. McKay; Library of Congress contributor, IA `harnessmaking01hasl`.

- Metadata: https://archive.org/metadata/harnessmaking01hasl
- Full OCR actually read: https://archive.org/stream/harnessmaking01hasl/harnessmaking01hasl_djvu.txt
- Preface as OCR read is signed P. N. Hasluck, La Belle Sauvage, London, May 1904. It is a US-published manual with British workshop context, not a catalog proving Chicago equipment selection.
- Metadata has no explicit rights/license field. US public-domain assessment rests on its 1904 publication; this is the audit's conclusion, not a LoC grant. Recheck imprint before image-derived use. No illustrations were derived here.
- Contents read: cart harness p.62; cart collars p.75; cart saddles/reins p.86; fore gear/leader harness p.101; plough harness p.107; bits/spurs/stirrups/furniture p.111; van/cab harness p.127.
- Text read on leather cutting distinguishes firm strap areas and specially curried leather for cart-saddle housings/winkers, because ordinary harness leather is not firm enough and contains too much oil. This supports varied stiffness/thickness and surface response across parts; one undifferentiated black strap material is insufficient.
- Printed **p.82** OCR describes matching the two stuffed collar sides in shape/size, smoothing lumps, joining the upper portions, and a soft-leather cap covering stitches. This supports a built/stitched/stuffed collar volume rather than a rigid seamless torus. It does not supply this project's horse's exact collar dimensions.
- Printed **p.119** OCR says brass furniture occurs in different qualities/finishes and partly leather-covered plated/brass fittings also occur. Finishes should follow the selected harness recipe, not default chrome.
- Late van/cab discussion states four-wheeler/hansom harness is larger/heavier than gig harness except at saddle, and describes ornamental harness separately. Do not merge all vehicle harness families.
- Plate/figure geometry is **unseen**; figure-to-page list and construction text identify the next exact retrieval targets. The manual is dated in 1904, so using it for a specific early-1904 scene needs publication timing considered; using it as direct 1835 gear evidence is disallowed.

## Acquired and read: locomotion/mechanics

**J. D. B. Stillman, The horse in motion …**, Boston: James R. Osgood and Company, **1882**, executed/published under Leland Stanford; Muybridge credited for photography. IA `horseinmotionass00stil`, Webster Family Library of Veterinary Medicine copy.

- Metadata: https://archive.org/metadata/horseinmotionass00stil
- Full OCR actually read: https://archive.org/stream/horseinmotionass00stil/horseinmotionass00stil_djvu.txt
- Title/imprint and copyright text as OCR read: 1882 publication, copyright 1881 by Leland Stanford. Metadata has no explicit rights/license field. US public-domain assessment is based on its US publication before 1931, not a host-specific license statement.
- Preface read describes Muybridge experiments increasing to 24 cameras and discusses instantaneous limb positions. This establishes the source's photographic mechanics project, not exact durations for an animation made here.
- Printed **pp.52–53** OCR discusses varying load-related pastern/fetlock deflection and suspensory ligament behavior. Some blanket historical physiological statements require modern cross-check; this is not a veterinary authority for safety or health. Modeling consequence: locked straight fetlocks throughout support are a poor mechanically informed fixture; distinguish neutral support from loaded gait poses.
- Trot discussion read describes alternating diagonal legs and torso response. First fixture should use alternating diagonal contact pairs and continuous ground contact evaluation rather than copying the camera's interface gait labels.
- Contents/plate list read names **LIV Horse Hauling**, **LV Irregular Hauling**, and **LVII–CV Illustrations of the Paces**, plus **CVI** camera arrangement and **CVII** background/stride measurement. OCR captions include walking, trotting and cantering. These are exact targets for visual acquisition; none of those plates was viewed in this pass, and no plate-specific footfall sequence/speed/stride measurement is claimed.
- Application boundary: late-19th-century equine mechanics can inform anatomy/locomotion independent of scene date, explicitly labeled; it cannot establish a particular horse, harness, breed or service in 1835 Chicago.
