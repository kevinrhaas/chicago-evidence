## Method and honest coverage

This was image inspection, not a live browser interaction test. - Pre-fire: all 24 registered media records visually screened, including maps; 3 promising/contextual images also inspected at full size (Wolf Point NYPL, Kurz & Allison sheet, Green Tree). Complete registry screening does not mean every map street was magnified or every archive original was accessed.
- Prairie: all 944 source records searched for horse/wagon/carriage/coach terms and streetscape IDs; 83 available local images selected and visually screened. Six transport candidates inspected full size, listed below. This is a targeted pass, not an exhaustive visual audit of all 944 records; 861 records remain outside this visual pass, including text and unavailable-image records. Additional uncaptioned transport may occur in them.
- Duplicates/crops are not independent corroboration. Approximate dates and unresolved rights are preserved. No dimensions, breeds, colors, makers, or owners inferred from monochrome pictures.

## Pre-fire findings and cautions

No dependable contemporary photographic carriage/horse modeling reference was identified in the 24-image screening. The collection is predominantly maps and later reconstructions of early Chicago, not a street-life photographic census. This is an evidence gap, not evidence that horses or wagons were absent.

- `MEDIA-BLDG-KURZ-ALLISON-EARLY-DAYS`: the 1893 retrospective sheet visibly includes small horse vehicles by the court house (panel 6), Rush Medical College (panel 4), and water works (panel 7), and mounted riders by Green Tree (panel 11) and Sauganash (panel 14). They depict earlier dates but were created decades later. Useful as an illustration inventory only; no exact tack, anatomy, paint or vehicle-type authority. Source: <https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2003656685/>; recorded public domain.
- `MEDIA-BLDG-WOLFPOINT-NYPL-1867`: a horse appears on the tavern sign; people, canoes and a bridge are visible. Do not count the sign silhouette as an observed live horse or actual carriage. 1867 lithograph after a 1832 drawing, depicting 1833. Source: <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wolf%27s_Point_in_1833_(NYPL_Hades-118496-54620).tif>; recorded public domain.
- `MEDIA-BLDG-GREEN-TREE-1859`: **source-type conflict**. Viewer metadata says `historical_photograph`, but the actual local image visibly has continuous engraved/hatching lines throughout building, sky and ground. No horse/wagon visible. Do not cite as a direct 1859 photograph without checking original catalog and image lineage. Source: <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Green_Tree_Tavern_Chicago_1859.jpg>; metadata calls public domain while requesting original-item terms review.
- Perspective maps (1857 Palmatary, 1868 Ruger, 1871 views) offer city context; at screened resolution they do not supply defensible transport construction detail. Full archival-resolution map examination remains separate work.

## Modeling and animation implications

Start the historical dossiers with Harvey-frontage brougham-type carriage, J. P. Smith & Co ice wagon, and the disputed-location horse/gear photograph. Seek museum object records, measured carriage-builder plans and additional elevations to close hidden-side, suspension, axle, pole/shaft, hitch and harness questions. A single image cannot deliver a photographically faithful complete 3D reconstruction.

Separate pre-1871 and 1880s–1904 variants. The Prairie photographs cannot silently authorize the same vehicle/livery/tack in pre-fire scenes. Document any cross-period analogy explicitly. Horse breeds and coat colors remain unresolved in these photographs. Riding tack requires its own saddle/bridle evidence; carriage harness is not riding equipment.

Preserve future animation requirements from the first model: independently rotating wheels and steering forecarriage, correctly positioned shaft/pole and hitch anchors, horse neck/limb/hoof articulation, harness attachment constraints, driver/passenger seat anchors, doors and boarding steps. Static evidence approval should precede moving distant traffic, then mounted riders/drivers, then boarding/riding interactions. A reference pass is not proof of a production-quality animated asset.
