Finding
The apparent 27-versus-21 discrepancy is fully explained by the current plan's
explicit omissions. The report's p.49 table contains 27 named bridge rows. The
plan renders 21 of those rows and already records the other six as omissions:
Erie, Twenty-second, Main, Fuller, Archer, and Western. Its separately map-attested
Division Street canal crossing brings the rendered road-crossing count to 22.
The report count is not evidence of six newly discovered unidentified crossings,
a baseline counting error, or a need to add railway/private bridges.

Directly inspected primary evidence
Source: Tenth Annual Report of the Board of Public Works to the Common Council
of the City of Chicago, for the municipal fiscal year ending March 31, 1871.
Imprint: University Press, Cambridge, Mass.: Welch, Bigelow, & Co., 1871.
Item: https://archive.org/details/annualreportboa06workgoog

Printed p.48 (endpoint n58):
https://archive.org/download/annualreportboa06workgoog/page/n58.jpg
"The city bridges crossing the river are 27 in number"; all are described as
truss bridges revolving upon a centre pier. The text expressly introduces p.49
as the table of pedestrian and vehicle crossings observed March 31, 1871, from
6 a.m. to 7 p.m. The new Twenty-second Street bridge is among the past year's work.

Printed p.49 (endpoint n59):
https://archive.org/download/annualreportboa06workgoog/page/n59.jpg
"Tabular Statement Concerning Bridges" contains 27 named rows. The accompanying
named-row-reconciliation.json preserves printed row order, expanded material
dittos, and the matching rendered plan ID or explicit omission. Counting its
descriptions yields 16 wooden-brace/iron-chord bridges, 1 all-iron bridge
(Madison), and 10 wooden-brace/wooden-chord bridges. No anonymous railway/private
supplement is needed to make 27. This pass did not recheck traffic arithmetic,
dimensions, builder names, or the entire geographic alignment.

Printed p.112 (endpoint n122):
https://archive.org/download/annualreportboa06workgoog/page/n122.jpg
The Streets and Bridges report is dated Chicago, April 1, 1871, and expressly
covers the year ending March 31, 1871. It states there are "twenty-seven pivot
bridges" upon the main river and branches: 16 with iron chords, wooden braces,
and iron turntables; 1 entirely iron; and 10 wooden truss and wooden turntable.
These categories agree numerically with the 27-row table. The different page
locations do not represent a later October inventory or an added railway census.

Printed p.113 (endpoint n123):
https://archive.org/download/annualreportboa06workgoog/page/n123.jpg
The continuation lists stone abutments at Van Buren, a new bridge over the
Illinois and Michigan Canal at Western Avenue, a new Erie bridge, and a new
South Branch bridge at Twenty-second. It also reports South Halsted unsafe for
heavy travel and recommends replacement; Wells and Clybourn are expected to do
service one or two years longer. These are the superintendent's spring condition
assessments, not established October closures or October repair outcomes.

Its bridge_inventory_note expressly calls the 21 report-backed + 1 map-backed

Erie and Twenty-second are omitted because the report says "Not in use" at the
March 31 count and their October opening is unresolved. Main, Fuller, Archer,
and Western are omitted because their period-map river/canal placements have

uncertainty is not a seventh omission from the 27-row report table. Railway
crossings remain outside this particular plan-road-inventory comparison.

Remaining limits, preserved rather than silently resolved
1. Erie and Twenty-second: construction in 1871 and "new bridge" prose do not
   establish that they were open to road traffic on October 8. The table's
   "Not in use" is anchored to the March 31 traffic-count context.
2. Main, Fuller, Archer, Western: names are directly attested on p.49; this pass
   supplies no coordinates or additional placement confidence.
3. Western has a source-description nuance: p.49 labels its location "W. fork
   of S. Branch" and construction year 1869, while p.113 describes a new Western
   Avenue bridge over the Illinois and Michigan Canal among the past year's
   improvements. Whether these descriptions refer to the same structure,
   replacement work, or another nearby crossing requires further source work.
   Do not infer a 28th bridge or silently choose modern geography. The table's
   Western traffic entry is "No account", not "Not in use".
4. Matching 27 rows to the report narratives resolves this count question. It
   does not prove a complete inventory of every city, private, railway, park,
   canal, or October 1871 crossing, nor that all 27 were then open for traffic.
5. Existing notebook bridge records warn that their dense traffic transcription
   and printed traffic totals have not been independently reconciled. This
   named-row/material count pass does not clear that separate limitation.
