Chicago Avenue and Pine Street: bounded 1871 utility-corner research
Scans inspected 19 November 2026. Read-only follow-up for a future small PR; no application, ticket or Git changes.

RESULT
The original report supports one new hydrant at the northeast corner of historic Chicago avenue and Pine street during the year ending April 1, 1871. It supports the practice of putting street names on city lamps, but does not name a lamp at this corner. Hydrant casting, dimensions, paint, outlet orientation, curb setback, service condition and the appearance or placement of a corner lamp remain unestablished. Bringing the reported installation forward to October 8 is a continuity inference, not a witnessed condition at 16:00.

HYDRANT: WHAT THE PRINTED PAGES SAY
P.79 introduces the list:
“During the year ending April 1, 1871, the following new hydrants were established, viz.:—”
P.84, under NORTH DIVISION, includes:
“One northeast corner Chicago avenue and Pine street.”
The immediately following, separate entry is:
“One on Chicago avenue at the Gund Engine House.”

The two entries are separate locations; do not merge the corner hydrant with the engine-house installation. “New hydrants” is a subset of installations in a stated reporting year, not a complete inventory of every hydrant then present. The recorded corner is historic Pine Street, not the curb geometry of today's widened Michigan Avenue. This pass has not solved an exact historical curb coordinate, parcel line or hydrant offset.

STREET NAMES ON LAMPS: WHAT THE PRINTED PAGES SAY
P.112 dates J. K. Thompson's Streets and Bridges report “Chicago, April 1, 1871” and states that it covers the year ending March 31, 1871. P.116 is its concluding section, followed by his signature as Superintendent of Streets and Bridges.

P.116 reads:
“The plan recently adopted by the Board, of placing the names of streets upon the lamps of the city, is worthy of commendation, and seems to give general satisfaction. I know of but one objection to the plan, and that is, the liability of the lamps being so changed as to place the names on the wrong streets. A little care on the part of those having charge of cleaning and repairing the lamps would entirely overcome this objection.”

This records an adopted practice and a maintenance concern. It supplies no complete installation list, coverage guarantee, lettering style, dimensions, attachment method, paint colour, lamp maker, burner design or corner-specific placement. It does not say every corner was labeled, nor that a mislabeled lamp stood at Chicago/Pine. A modeled street-name lamp at that corner would therefore be a separate, explicitly reconstructed choice. No fire response, lamplighting, repair work or other action at the scene time follows from the text.
