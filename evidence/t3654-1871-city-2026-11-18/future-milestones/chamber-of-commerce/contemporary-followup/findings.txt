RESULT
The printed Andreas height contradiction remains unresolved. A contemporary description now supplies a directly inspected 100-foot building-height claim, but it does not identify the lower datum or specify cornice versus roof endpoint. Do not turn it into a measured elevation, combine it arithmetically with Andreas, or silently choose one of Andreas's two incompatible 99-foot statements.

WHAT THE SCAN ACTUALLY SAYS
Page 9, left column, third main paragraph:
"The dimensions of the main building are ninety-three by one hundred and eighty-one feet, and one hundred feet in height."
The same page locates it at the southeast corner of LaSalle and Washington, says the sides face LaSalle and Exchange Place, and describes stairs from the main door and a mansard roof. None of these sentences states whether the 100 feet is measured from the street, first floor, basement floor, cornice or top of roof. Page 10 identifies architect E. Burling and the dedication on Wednesday, August 30, 1865, confirming this is the original pre-fire building rather than a later replacement.

PRESERVED COMPARISON
- Andreas p.358: roof extreme height 23 feet above cornice and 99 feet from basement floor; next sentence also places cornice 99 feet from basement floor. This remains a real printed internal contradiction.
- Chicago Illustrated page 9: main building 100 feet in height, with no datum or endpoint specified. This is a separate historical report, not proof that either Andreas height sentence is correct.
- Depth: Andreas explicitly gives 180 feet; Chicago Illustrated explicitly gives 181 feet. Both report 93-foot frontage. Preserve 180-versus-181 as a source disagreement; do not overwrite either or confuse it with historical map-registration uncertainty.
- Other differences are also visible: Chicago Illustrated gives the hall as 143 × 89 × 44 feet and mentions eighteen windows, whereas Andreas gives 143 × 87 × 45 feet and ten windows on each side plus five at the north. This pass does not adjudicate those differences or assemble a hybrid room from them.

BOUNDED SEARCH LIMITS
The Chicagology transcription of Chicago Tribune, August 29, 1865 describes roof space about 22 feet above the ceiling joists, but the original newspaper page was not acquired here and this is not an absolute cornice/roof datum. It therefore supplies only a lead, not a resolution. The 1865 Annual Review of the Trade and Commerce of Chicago was searched in OCR; its Chamber entry supplies dimensions but no useful absolute height. It was not promoted to visually verified height evidence. No further search was pursued after the contemporary Chicago Illustrated page was inspected.

TENANCY / ACTIVITY LIMIT
Signboards in the lithograph or the later Andreas engraving establish only that those labels are represented in those views. Their presence alone does not establish a firm's tenancy, sign survival, office operation or an individual person's presence on October 8, 1871. Use an appropriate 1871 directory or other dated record before adopting tenancy; the pictured street figures and vehicles likewise do not document that day's actions.

HANDOFF
Carry the 100-foot undatumed height report and 180/181-foot depth disagreement into the later landmark's research/discrepancy record. Keep absolute vertical dimensions unresolved until stronger evidence or an explicit, reasoned inference is available. The new scan route materially improves reference-image readiness, but it does not itself settle height or make the future model exact.
